Moderne CLI — designing for the command line

Bringing product-design rigor to a developer command-line tool — onboarding, tool discovery, and ways to observe and trust what automated runs are doing, for humans and AI agents alike.

Role
Product designer — developer-tool UX
Context
Enterprise developer-tools platform
Focus
Onboarding → discovery → observability
Collaboration
Engineering, developer relations

The problem

The tooling was powerful but assumed you were already an expert. Getting started ("day zero") was steep, discovering tools was hard, and automated runs were opaque. And increasingly, the "user" wasn't only a person at a terminal. Designing for a CLI means designing for trust and speed, not screens.

Constraints

  • Two kinds of user. The experience had to work for humans and AI agents reading and acting on the same output.
  • Respect the terminal. Developers expect speed, text, and conventions — not a GUI bolted onto a CLI.
  • Make runs observable. Automated work has to be legible enough to trust.
  • Meet developers where they are. Fit existing workflows instead of demanding new ones.

Observability

Making automated work legible

The heart of the work was observability: when a tool runs, you should be able to follow the trace — what ran, in what order, and what happened — whether you're a developer scanning output or an agent reasoning over it. Legibility is the trust.

Illustrative — recreated for portfolio; placeholder commands, not the production CLI

Key decisions & tradeoffs

  • Agents as first-class users. Designing output to be legible to AI agents, not just humans, shaped structure and wording throughout.
  • Observability over magic. Showing the trace costs some brevity but buys the trust automation needs.
  • Guidelines for consistency. Design guidelines kept the tool coherent as it grew across commands.

Outcomes

A gentler day zero

Onboarding guidance lowered the barrier to a first productive run.

Discoverable tools

A tool browser made a powerful, growing toolbox understandable.

Trustworthy runs

Observation and trace prototypes shaped how automated work is shown and trusted.

What it taught me

When your users include AI agents, legibility is the whole product.
