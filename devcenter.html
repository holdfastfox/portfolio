DevCenter — engineering-health reporting

Turning scattered customer feedback into a clear reporting product — and rebuilding it from a monolithic template into a modular app.

Role
Product designer — UX research, data visualization
Context
Enterprise developer-tools platform
Focus
Research → product direction → rebuild
Collaboration
Engineering, product, customers

The problem

Engineering-health reporting was hard to read, built on an unwieldy monolithic template. Customers wanted to see the state of their codebase over time, but the product made insight feel like work — and the underlying build made it hard to change. Two-sided challenge: clearer experience, on a foundation that could actually evolve.

Constraints

  • Many voices, one direction. Feedback came from several enterprise customers with different priorities.
  • Data-dense, but legible. Reporting has to surface a lot without overwhelming.
  • Rebuild without breaking trust. Customers relied on the existing product while it changed underneath them.

How I worked

01

Research

Ran live feedback sessions with several enterprise customers. One recorded walkthrough directly drove three design changes: cards that scale with repository count, a drill-down from summary card to underlying repos, and a clearer completed state.

02

Design

Designed a time-dimension view, plain-language metric explainers, and a safe preview path before committing. One explainer was designed in direct response to customer confusion over how a specific metric was calculated — a proactive answer built into the UI.

03

Rebuild

Partnered with engineering to rebuild from a monolithic template into a modular app, shipping incrementally.

Making the data make sense

Two moves did most of the work: adding a time dimension so teams could see trends rather than a single snapshot, and pairing every metric with a plain-language explainer so people trusted what they were looking at. The explainers weren't a nice-to-have. One came directly from a customer who couldn't tell how a metric was calculated — the explanation went into the UI so no one had to ask.

Illustrative — recreated for portfolio; not the production UI or real customer data

Key decisions & tradeoffs

  • Trends over snapshots. The time dimension answered the question customers actually had: are we getting better?
  • Explainers build trust. Spelling out what each metric means cost space but turned skepticism into reliance.
  • Dry-run before commit. A safe preview path lowered the risk of acting on a report.

Outcomes

Shipped and shared immediately

The timeline view shipped to several enterprise tenants. Internal teams asked for documentation the same day — they wanted to show it to their own accounts.

Rebuilt to ship faster

The modular rebuild replaced a monolithic template. New views and metrics can now be added without touching the whole app.

Research drove the design

A recorded customer walkthrough changed the card layout, added a drill-down path, and clarified the completed state — not in a later iteration, but before anything shipped.

What it taught me

Data only helps when people trust and understand it — the explainer is part of the design, not an afterthought.
