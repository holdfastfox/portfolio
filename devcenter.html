DevCenter — engineering-health reporting

Turning scattered customer feedback into a clear reporting product — and rebuilding it from a monolithic template into a modular app.

Role
Product designer — UX research, data visualization
Context
Enterprise developer-tools platform
Focus
Research → product direction → rebuild
Collaboration
Engineering, product, customers

The problem

Engineering-health reporting was hard to read and built on an unwieldy monolithic template. Customers wanted to understand the state of their codebase over time, but the product made insight feel like work — and the underlying build made it hard to change. The challenge was two-sided: a clearer experience, on a foundation that could actually evolve.

Constraints

  • Many voices, one direction. Feedback came from several enterprise customers with different priorities.
  • Data-dense, but legible. Reporting has to surface a lot without overwhelming.
  • Rebuild without breaking trust. Customers relied on the existing product while it changed underneath them.

How I worked

01

Research

Synthesized feedback from several enterprise customers into a single, prioritized product direction.

02

Design

Designed a time-dimension view, plain-language metric explainers, and a safe "dry-run" path to preview before committing.

03

Rebuild

Partnered with engineering to rebuild from a monolithic template into a modular app, shipping incrementally.

Making the data make sense

Two moves did most of the work: adding a time dimension so teams could see trends rather than a single snapshot, and pairing every metric with a plain-language explainer so people trusted what they were looking at.

Key decisions & tradeoffs

  • Trends over snapshots. A time dimension added complexity but answered the question customers actually had: "are we getting better?"
  • Explainers build trust. Spelling out what each metric means cost space but turned skepticism into reliance.
  • Dry-run before commit. A safe preview path lowered the risk of acting on a report.

Outcomes

Shipped to production

The time-dimension view and metric explainers went live in the product.

A foundation that evolves

The modular rebuild replaced a brittle template and made future change tractable.

Direction from many

Turned several customers' feedback into one coherent, shippable direction.

What it taught me

Data only helps when people trust and understand it — the explainer is part of the design, not an afterthought.
