01

The idea

Annie Rimbach turns the complexity of enterprise software into clear, humane products. She works at the frontier of agent experience (AX) — starting from the real user problem, designing the whole system rather than the screen, and shipping with curiosity intact.

The brand has to do two jobs at once: read credible enough for large enterprises, and unmistakably hers — warm, curious, and process-minded. Serious work, done with a sense of play.

05

The highlight

With the ribbon retired, the signature device is simpler and more useful: a lime marker swipe that highlights exactly one idea per view. It does double duty — it's the brand's pop of color, and it literally "shows the working" by marking what matters. Curious, process-minded, unmistakably yours.

Don't — too many

Rules: one highlight per screen · short phrases only · never on links or body paragraphs · always lime on the sage off-white, or lime behind deep-green text in dark mode.

06

Line work

You don't have to pick one — they're one family. Every line shares the same DNA: a 1px deep-green hairline with a single lime node or segment. What changes is the job each one does.

The discipline is restraint: at most one line element per view, plus at most one highlight. Texture lives in the background; accents mark a moment.

07

Voice & tone

Show the workingMake the thinking visible. Lead with the problem and the decisions, not the polish.
Clear over cleverPlain language about complex things. A little wit is welcome; jargon is not.
Curious, not certainAsk the better question. Frame work as exploration, not proclamation.
Real problems firstAlways tie design back to a real user and a real outcome.
Sounds like
"Here's the problem we actually needed to solve, the three bets we made, and what shipped taught us."
Not like
"Leveraging synergistic AI paradigms to revolutionize the enterprise at scale."
08

Portfolio direction

A headline-led homepage: name in the nav, the idea front and center, then work as a calm, numbered index. Case studies are framed as a process — problem, decisions, tradeoffs, what it taught me — so the page argues your thinking before it shows a single screen.
