Kohl's — designing the self-checkout experience

Establishing Kohl's first in-house SCO design practice — in-store user research, UI redesign to brand standards, and integrating Kohl's Cash and rewards into the transaction flow.

Role
Design lead — Self-Checkout (SCO) team
Context
Retail enterprise · in-store kiosk experience
Focus
Research → UI redesign → design system
Impact
8 stores with SCO on day one → 275 today

The situation

When I joined, the SCO team had no in-house designer — design had been handled by a third party with no user research. The result: confusing interactions, transaction abandonment, and a UI that didn't follow Kohl's design standards.

Three problems stacked up: bad UX, brand non-compliance, and two major revenue programs (Kohl's Cash, Kohl's Credit) barely integrated into the flow.

Three tracks running in sequence: establish the research practice, redesign the UI, and integrate the revenue programs that had been left out.

Research — the hardware is the constraint

Before me, all user testing happened in browsers. Self-checkout kiosks are fixed touchscreen hardware with physical constraints browsers can't replicate — reach distance, touch targets, glare, scanning angles.

I set up the first in-store observational and hardware testing. What we found: almost all rewards interaction load was falling on self-checkout.

The SCO experience was carrying nearly all of the rewards interaction load for the entire store — and it was failing customers at exactly that moment. This shaped everything about how we prioritized the redesign.

UI redesign & design system

I rebuilt the UI from the ground up to meet Kohl's design standards — the look, the interaction patterns, the visual language. The previous third-party work had departed from the brand significantly; bringing it back to standard was as much a trust and brand-coherence issue as a usability one.

The SCO environment is touchscreen-only, and Kohl's had no touchscreen-specific components. I built the first set — sized for touch targets, designed for the hardware — and added them to the Kohl's design library.

Kohl's Cash & rewards integration

Kohl's Cash and Kohl's Credit weren't properly surfaced at self-checkout. I worked with the PM to reprioritize — grounding the change in what research had shown.

The full rewards flow I designed covered:

  • Yes2You rewards lookup — by phone number and zip code, at the start of the transaction
  • Kohl's Cash application — in the checkout sidebar so customers could use existing balances
  • Coupon scanning and entry — scan physical coupons or type in offer codes
  • Kohl's Card prompt — a well-timed "Save 35% when you open a Kohl's Card" offer at checkout, with QR-code application flow

The PM's goal: increase SCO adoption to reduce staffing costs. Integrating rewards was the unlock — customers with Kohl's Cash had a reason to choose self-checkout.

What it taught me

The hardware is the design constraint. Until you test on it, you don't know what you're designing.

Browser testing can tell you a lot — but a kiosk's physical reality (reach distance, touch targets, retail floor noise and light) only shows up when you're standing in the store. Every assumption got revised once we watched real customers use real machines.
